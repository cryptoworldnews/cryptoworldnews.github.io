NYSE Links Tokenized Stock Clearing to Avalanche

Ava Labs says Avalanche will power clearing for an NYSE-linked tokenized stock venue, with planned 24/7 trading and settlement subject to approval.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 10, 2026 · 09:15 UTC#98e7133 min read

Ava Labs said on Nov. 9 that Avalanche will provide the core ledger for clearing trades on an upcoming NYSE-affiliated venue for tokenized securities, a move that puts blockchain technology into the post-trade machinery of a major stock-market operator. The Ava Labs announcement says the platform’s trading, clearing and settlement plans are subject to regulatory approval.

The platform is an alternative trading system, or ATS, affiliated with the New York Stock Exchange. Its design separates the job of matching buyers and sellers from the job of recording and settling their trades. The exchange’s Pillar system is expected to handle order matching, while a blockchain ledger will support clearing and settlement, according to CoinDesk’s report.

What role will Avalanche play in each trade?

Avalanche’s ledger will support the clearing firm behind the ATS. After the venue matches an order, the clearing process confirms the obligations created by the trade; settlement records the transfer of the asset and payment. The ledger is intended to record and settle those trades. It is like a shared record book, but the firms running the market control who can participate and how the system operates.

The setup is planned to use a custom blockchain built with Avalanche technology, rather than the public Avalanche network itself, Ava Labs chief business officer John Nahas told CoinDesk. The announcement says the platform is designed for trading and settlement around the clock. That is a stated aim, not a live service: regulatory approval remains a condition.

Will tokenized shares have to start on Avalanche?

No. NYSE head of digital issuance Dennis Lee said in the release that the platform is meant to support equities listed on a range of blockchains. Avalanche’s role is the ledger for the clearing infrastructure, where trades are recorded and settled. The announcement does not describe how the platform will connect each supported blockchain to that ledger.

Tokenization represents a security as a digital token on a blockchain. In this planned venue, the tokens could originate across multiple networks, while the clearing ledger provides the record for completed trades. The release says the system is being built for fast, reliable settlement, predictable costs and control over participation, but gives no launch date or operating figures.

What does this change for stock-market infrastructure?

The announcement extends NYSE’s earlier plan for a separate digital platform to trade and settle tokenized securities. The exchange’s parent, Intercontinental Exchange, said that plan would support 24/7 trading, instant settlement and stablecoin-based funding, as reported by CoinDesk. The Avalanche selection names a technology provider for one part of that design: the ledger supporting clearing.

For now, the development is an infrastructure choice, not evidence that tokenized stocks are already trading on the venue. The platform still needs regulatory approval, and the announcement leaves its launch timing and the process for connecting multiple blockchains unspecified.

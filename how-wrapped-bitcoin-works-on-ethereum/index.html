How wrapped bitcoin works on Ethereum

Wrapped bitcoin gives BTC an ERC-20 form on Ethereum, backed by reserves but dependent on an issuer, a redemption route and market demand to hold its price near BTC.

Merkle Street NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 05:32 UTC#be9a4c4 min read

Wrapped bitcoin is a token on Ethereum that represents bitcoin held elsewhere, letting BTC move through Ethereum applications without moving the original coins off the Bitcoin network. In the common custodial model, a merchant accepts BTC, a custodian holds it, and a smart contract issues an equivalent amount of WBTC. Think of it as a claim ticket: the ticket can circulate, but its value depends on the asset held behind it and the process for exchanging it back.

The parts do different jobs. The Bitcoin network records the original BTC deposit. The custodian controls the reserve. An approved merchant handles customer requests to mint or redeem. The Ethereum contract creates, transfers and burns WBTC. For a comparison of RenBridge routes, fees and timing, see the separate guide. Those details matter because each route has its own steps and costs.

How does bitcoin become WBTC on Ethereum?

A merchant starts the minting process with the custodian. The customer sends BTC to an address provided for the transaction, and the custodian waits for the Bitcoin payment to be confirmed. Once the deposit is verified, the custodian authorizes the Ethereum contract to mint the matching amount of WBTC to the merchant. The merchant then passes the WBTC to the customer.

After that, WBTC behaves like an ERC-20 token. A holder can send it to another Ethereum address or use it in applications that accept the token. A decentralized exchange can trade it against another token; a lending protocol can accept it as collateral. These actions move WBTC on Ethereum. They do not move the reserve BTC or create a Bitcoin transaction.

That is the key distinction: WBTC brings bitcoin’s market exposure into Ethereum’s transaction system, rather than making the Bitcoin network itself run Ethereum contracts. The token is useful wherever an application supports it, but it is a separate asset with its own contract and redemption arrangements.

How do you redeem WBTC for bitcoin?

Redemption reverses the minting steps. The holder sends WBTC to a participating merchant and provides a Bitcoin address. The merchant submits the tokens for burning, which removes them from circulation. After the burn is verified, the custodian releases the corresponding BTC to the specified address. Direct minting and redemption may be limited to approved merchants; other holders usually acquire or sell WBTC through a market or exchange.

The route back can involve several costs and waits. A direct redemption may include merchant or custodian fees and a Bitcoin network fee. Buying or selling through a market adds trading fees or a spread, while moving WBTC on Ethereum requires gas. The exact cost and timing depend on the route, network conditions and the service handling the exchange.

What keeps WBTC near the price of bitcoin?

The intended backing is one BTC for each WBTC in circulation. When new WBTC is minted against deposited BTC, supply increases; when WBTC is redeemed and burned, supply falls and BTC leaves custody. This process gives authorized participants a way to connect the token’s supply to the reserve.

That link does not guarantee that WBTC trades at exactly the same price as BTC at every moment. Market demand, available trading liquidity, redemption access and confidence in the custodian can all affect its price. A buyer should check that the token contract and network match the WBTC they intend to use, then consider how they could sell or redeem it later.

  • Contract risk: Ethereum applications rely on the token contract and the code that interacts with it.
  • Custody risk: The reserve depends on the custodian holding and releasing the BTC as promised.
  • Access risk: A holder may need an approved merchant or a liquid market to convert WBTC back to BTC.
  • Network costs: Ethereum gas, Bitcoin fees and service charges can make small transfers uneconomical.

For most people who only want BTC exposure, holding BTC avoids the extra Ethereum contract and custody layers. WBTC makes sense when a specific Ethereum application needs a tokenized form of bitcoin. Its convenience comes from that compatibility; its trade-off is reliance on the reserve, issuer and route that connect the token to BTC.

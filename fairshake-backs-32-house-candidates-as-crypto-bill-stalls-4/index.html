Fairshake backs 32 House candidates as crypto bill stalls

Fairshake named 32 House candidates for support, with $1 million each for six, as the crypto industry seeks allies after Senate action on its market-structure bill stalled.

Merkle Street NewsroomOct 10, 2026 · 12:18 UTC#56d7e23 min read

Fairshake is backing 32 House candidates in November’s midterms, giving $1 million in planned spending to six, as the crypto industry looks to revive market-structure legislation that stalled in the Senate. The Associated Press report on Fairshake’s slate says the list has 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats; all supported legislation that was a top industry priority.

How does Fairshake’s campaign spending reach a race?

Fairshake is registered with the Federal Election Commission as a monthly Super PAC, or independent-expenditure-only committee. The process works in steps: the group chooses a candidate to support or oppose, pays for election activity such as advertising, and reports that spending to the FEC. The money does not go directly to the candidate. The FEC describes independent expenditures as spending in support of or opposition to a candidate, with no funds given directly to or spent by that candidate.

Think of it as a separate megaphone beside a campaign: the group can pay to put its own message before voters, while the candidate’s campaign remains a separate operation. The FEC’s Fairshake committee record identifies the committee type and tracks its reported receipts and spending.

Which House candidates get the largest commitments?

Fairshake said it plans to spend $1 million each supporting three Democrats: Reps. Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California. Three Republicans will receive the same level of support: Reps. French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. Fairshake did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 House candidates.

The slate comes alongside a larger Senate commitment. Fairshake announced in September that it planned to spend nearly $30 million opposing Democrat Sherrod Brown in Ohio, where he is challenging Republican Sen. Jon Husted. AP reported that Fairshake and affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash at the end of August.

What stalled, and what does Fairshake say comes next?

Senate Democrats blocked the CLARITY Act in September. The bill sought to establish a federal regulatory framework for digital assets and divide oversight between federal regulators. Opponents said it did not adequately address President Donald Trump’s crypto investments. The legislation’s future is now tied to the next Congress: Fairshake says it is supporting candidates who back crypto policy, and the industry hopes to revisit the stalled bill after the midterms.

Fairshake spokesperson Geoff Vetter told AP that the group and its affiliated PACs spent $40 million supporting 29 Democrats and $31 million supporting 28 Republicans in primaries and special elections this year. He said they won 53 of the 57 races they entered. The House slate spreads support across both parties, while its exact price remains undisclosed beyond the six $1 million commitments.

Do DEX Aggregators Charge Extra Fees: How to Read a Swap Quote

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 21:45 UTC#fe3cdd4 min read

A DEX aggregator takes your sell token and amount, finds a route across liquidity sources, and returns a quote for the token you want to receive. To examine the route, fee line and minimum received in a live quote, go to Matcha swap. Some aggregators add their own fee, depending on the venue, token pair or integrating app. The route may also use DEX pools that charge fees, while network gas adds a separate cost.

The Swap Quote Shows Which Costs Come From the Route

The swap quote starts with your input amount, prices the available routes and shows the resulting output. A route through a liquidity pool includes that pool’s trading fee in its quoted rate. Uniswap v3, for instance, has pools with different fee tiers for the same token pair. A route through several pools can involve several pool fees, even when the interface shows only one final rate.

  • Pool fee: The pool takes it during the swap, so it is already reflected in the quoted output. It usually is not a second charge to add to that output.
  • Aggregator or app fee: This is an additional charge when the venue or integrating app applies one. Look for its amount and payment token in the fee details. The 0x Swap API documentation shows how an app sets a fee in basis points and receives it in the quote’s fee data.
  • Gas: Network execution has a cost set by the work required and network demand. On a regular on-chain swap, your wallet pays it in the network’s native token, separately from the buy-token amount.
  • Price impact: A larger order can move a pool’s price and reduce output. That reduction is part of the trade price, not a payment to the aggregator. Slippage is different again: it describes how far execution may move from the quote.

Say you sell 1,000 USDC and one route quotes 500 buy tokens. Its pool fee is already in that 500-token figure. An app fee, if charged, needs to be accounted for in the displayed output or fee breakdown; gas may still leave your wallet separately. Treating the embedded pool fee as another deduction would count it twice. Treating price impact as an aggregator fee would blame the venue for a change caused by the order’s size and available liquidity.

For a Matcha swap quote, read the route, any fee line and minimum received together. The route explains where the trade executes; the fee line identifies an added charge; minimum received is the output floor allowed by your slippage setting. The Matcha Help Center explains how its swap fees and gas treatment vary by trading mode and token pair.

When comparing Matcha, 1inch, CoW Swap or another interface, enter the same tokens, amount and network. Compare minimum received after token-side fees, then subtract separately paid gas after converting it to the same value unit. A route quoting 500 tokens with a 495-token minimum and gas worth two tokens has a 493-token net floor. Another quoting 499 with a 497-token minimum and gas worth one has a 496-token net floor. The first headline rate looks better; the second gives the stronger minimum after gas.

Related Questions

Two related questions help explain changes in received amounts and quotes labelled gasless.

Why Is My Received Amount Lower Than the Quote?

Your received amount can be lower than the expected quote when the price moves before execution, within the slippage you allowed. Compare it with the transaction’s minimum received, then inspect the route and fee details. A worse pool price, an applied app fee and a separate gas payment appear in different parts of that calculation.

Are Gasless Swaps Really Free?

Gasless swaps remove the separate gas payment from your wallet, but the transaction still has a network cost. A service may sponsor it or build it into the trade’s quoted output. Compare the final amount you receive with a regular swap’s minimum received after gas; the word “gasless” alone does not tell you which route costs less.

HSBC starts retail rollout of HKD stablecoin RedCoin

HSBC has launched RedCoin on PayMe, giving users a way to send HKD stablecoins to people and merchants, with tokenised investments planned for a later phase.

Merkle Street NewsroomOct 26, 2026 · 12:19 UTC#ddbbc33 min read

HSBC launched its first Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, RedCoin (ticker RCHKD), on PayMe on 23 October, opening a route for customers to send digital HKD to people and merchants. The bank said more than 3.3 million PayMe users would be able to activate wallets in the app over the following days. The HSBC launch announcement says the coin is issued under a licence from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA).

How does RedCoin move between users?

A customer activates a RedCoin wallet inside PayMe, then uses it to send coins to another person or pay a participating merchant. HSBC says the app supports peer-to-peer transfers and payments at more than 100,000 merchants. The coin is HKD-denominated, fully backed by segregated, high-quality liquid assets and designed to be redeemable one-for-one for Hong Kong dollars, subject to the bank’s terms.

That backing is the part that connects the digital token to ordinary money: HSBC sets assets aside to match the value of coins in circulation. The bank also says HSBC Digital Assets Custody provides safekeeping for RedCoin. It has not specified in the launch announcement which blockchain network the coin uses, or whether customers can transfer it to wallets outside HSBC’s apps.

Who can use it, and what is available now?

The launch is phased. HSBC said PayMe users would be able to activate wallets in the days after 23 October, so the app’s full user base is not the same as the number of people already using RedCoin. The South China Morning Post’s launch report said users could activate a wallet without a minimum balance or an additional account-opening requirement.

For the first phase, the stated uses are payments through PayMe: sending RedCoin to other people and paying merchants. HSBC says staff pilots with nearly 100 users tested the customer journey from minting and wallet activation to live transfers. The bank has not announced transaction volumes or how many customers activated wallets during rollout.

What does HSBC plan to add next?

HSBC plans to add RedCoin wallets to its Hong Kong mobile banking app later this year and let customers use the coin to subscribe to tokenised investment products. The bank says those products are to include a forthcoming share class of its HKD money market fund. It also says it plans to expand into wholesale corporate and institutional uses, but has not given a launch date for those services.

For now, RedCoin’s main test is whether customers use a regulated bank-issued token for routine payments inside an existing app. Its initial reach is tied to PayMe’s user base, while investment products and wider institutional use remain future plans.

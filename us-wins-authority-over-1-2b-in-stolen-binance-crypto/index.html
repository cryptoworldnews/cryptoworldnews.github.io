US wins authority over $1.2B in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in Binance theft proceeds as tracing and recovery efforts continue.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 18, 2026 · 05:32 UTC#fa74f32 min read

The Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The authorization covers part of a larger pool that authorities and private-sector partners have frozen; it does not mean that all of those assets have been returned to victims.

On or about Oct. 29, members of the North Korean military known to the FBI as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance, according to the Justice Department’s announcement on Nov. 12. The department says government and private-sector efforts have since frozen more than $2.7 billion. The court-authorized amount—more than $1.2 billion—is part of that frozen total.

Here is how the recovery effort works. Investigators trace the stolen assets as they move between crypto addresses. They work with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to block transfers and freeze assets. The Justice Department says the thieves continue to move funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. A bridge moves assets between blockchain networks; a mixer can obscure the transaction trail. In simple terms, a freeze is a hold on funds while officials work through the legal process.

The court authorization gives the government authority to seize or restrain the identified assets. Those are legal steps toward taking control of property or keeping it from moving. They do not, by themselves, decide who ultimately gets the funds. The Associated Press reported the FBI’s announcement and said the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then considers who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets.

Tracing and forfeiture work continues

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen crypto are ongoing. It did not give a timetable for completing those steps or returning funds. The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating recovery work, the department said. It also said the FBI had issued an alert asking virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses TraderTraitor is using to launder the Binance funds.

The distinction matters: more than $2.7 billion has been frozen, while court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that pool. Neither figure describes a completed return to customers. For now, investigators are following the assets and trying to keep them within reach; a judge will determine any legitimate claims through the forfeiture process.

Chainflip says attacker stole 122.86 BTC in $12m vault raid

Chainflip says an attacker stole 122.86 BTC alongside ETH and stablecoins, lifting losses to about $12 million as the protocol offers a 10% return bounty.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 23, 2026 · 05:04 UTC#c550d23 min read

Chainflip’s vaults hold assets on different blockchains, and validators use shared signing keys to authorize transactions out of them. On Nov. 21, an attacker took 122.86 BTC from the Bitcoin vault and assets worth about $4.86 million from the Ethereum vault, bringing reported losses to about $12 million. In a message to the attacker, Chainflip Labs offered to let them keep 10% if they returned the rest by a stated deadline.

How did the attacker take funds from both vaults?

Chainflip’s vaults send funds when transactions are authorized by the protocol’s validator set. The Block reports that the vault signing key is held jointly by up to 150 validators, with 100 required to approve a transaction. Chainflip says it identified how the key was exposed and has prevented the same attack from happening again, but it has not published the precise mechanism.

The Bitcoin transfer happened first. According to The Block’s report, 122.86 BTC left the Bitcoin vault about 30 minutes before the Ethereum vault was drained. Chainflip’s message lists the full amounts taken on Nov. 21: 122.86137042 BTC, 520.2243399 ETH, 3,249,631.41 USDC and 758,141.33 USDT.

Chainflip says preparations included Tornado Cash funding on Nov. 11 and registration of a validator account on Nov. 20. The company says it traced the server behind that account. The Block reported that the stolen Ethereum assets were sent through Tornado Cash; as of Sunday afternoon, the stolen bitcoin remained at the attacker’s address.

Who bears the loss?

The theft came from Chainflip’s protocol vaults. Its founder, Simon Harman, said the losses were significant for liquidity providers, who supply assets to the pools used to carry out swaps. Chainflip had not yet set out a compensation plan. Harman said the team was working on ideas for compensation and expected to publish an incident report on Monday.

For users, the immediate issue is whether swaps and other services can resume safely. Chainflip’s website said services were temporarily unavailable, and Harman said he was relatively confident the protocol could restart despite the losses.

What does the bounty offer change?

Chainflip’s offer gives the attacker a way to return most of the funds while keeping 10% as a bounty. The message said that if 90% arrived at the listed return addresses by 18:38 UTC on Nov. 23, the company would treat the return as a white-hat recovery, ask no further questions and withdraw its reports and complaints to law enforcement.

That offer does not restore the missing assets on its own. The next steps depend on whether funds are returned, how Chainflip accounts for the losses and what its incident report says about the signing-key exposure. The protocol says it has closed the vulnerability, but the published disclosure does not yet explain the full exploit.

MoonPay seeks OCC charter for trust bank and stablecoin services

MoonPay has asked the OCC to charter a trust bank for custody, stablecoin issuance and redemption, reserve management, and agent conversion services.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 23, 2026 · 15:33 UTC#40ff083 min read

MoonPay has applied to put digital asset custody, stablecoin issuance and redemption, reserve management, and conversion services under a proposed national trust bank. The company’s application to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) is dated Nov. 17, 2026; The Block reported that the OCC received it on Nov. 18.

What would MoonPay National Trust Bank do?

The proposed bank would provide three groups of services: fiduciary custody, stablecoin services, and conversion as an agent. The filing describes the stablecoin work as issuance, redemption, and reserve management. These are proposed activities, not services the OCC has approved for the new bank.

The application seeks a charter for a non-depository national bank whose operations would be limited to trust company activities and related work. MoonPay says it is not seeking federal deposit insurance. Like a shop with a narrowly defined job, the proposed bank would be organized around custody and related financial services rather than general deposit-taking. The filing does not describe a plan for the bank to take customer deposits.

How would the stablecoin services fit together?

Issuance and redemption are the steps for putting a stablecoin into circulation and taking it back out. Reserve management is another named function in the plan. The filing groups those services together but does not publicly specify which stablecoin MoonPay would issue, what reserve assets it would use, or how customers would access the service.

Custody would be a separate part of the proposed business: the bank would hold digital assets in a fiduciary capacity. Conversion as an agent is the third service named in the filing. MoonPay has not publicly detailed which assets it would convert or the process customers would use. The application’s business plan and financial projections are listed as confidential exhibits, so those details are not in the public portion.

What happens to the application now?

The OCC must consider the charter request. The Block’s report on the filing says the services would be subject to OCC approval. The filing names the proposed institution MoonPay National Trust Bank and lists its main office in Miami Beach, Florida.

The application proposes a five-member board, with one director still to be determined, and names MoonPay chief executive Ivan Soto-Wright as interim president and CEO. Those are proposed arrangements in the application, not confirmation that the bank has opened. Until the OCC acts, the charter and the services described in it remain plans awaiting review.

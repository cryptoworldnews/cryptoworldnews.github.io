A new high-APY vault, or the lower rate you know?

Choosing a high APY new vault vs an established low APY vault? Usually keep the core in the established vault; test a new one only when its premium is native.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 00:22 UTC#a368ba4 min read

Usually, keep the core of your deposit in an established lower-APY Morpho vault. A new vault can earn a small slice when its extra yield comes from lending activity you accept and its Curator is one you trust.

Each vault pools one asset and allocates it across lending markets and other supported yield sources. The Curator sets the strategy, while the Allocator directs funds within it. When comparing Morpho vaults, separate the yield source from the vault’s age: an attractive headline rate can depend on incentives, collateral risk or a small TVL.

What should you compare before choosing a vault?

APY source: Split the new vault’s rate into native interest and rewards. Rewards can lift the headline APY while incentives last, then fade when they end. Native yield comes from lending activity, but it is not automatically safer: higher native APY usually reflects riskier collateral or a higher LLTV in the markets the vault lends to. Compare the parts, not just the displayed total.

Collateral and LLTV: A lower LLTV leaves more room before a borrower’s position can be liquidated; a higher one allows more borrowing against collateral and can increase risk. Look at the assets and LLTVs in the markets behind each vault. A new vault’s higher native yield may be compensation for taking more of that risk, while an established vault’s lower rate may come with a market mix you already understand.

TVL and dilution: Your deposit can change the rate you actually earn in a small vault. Estimate post-deposit APY by dividing the vault’s current yearly interest by its TVL plus your deposit. This is a rough, static estimate: it assumes current yearly interest holds as funds enter. Say a vault has little TVL and its yearly interest is modest; a large deposit expands the denominator, so your share of that interest is lower than the headline APY suggests. A larger established vault is less exposed to this particular dilution from one depositor.

Curator and timelock: Compare the Curator’s track record and the timelock length for changes. Cap increases and market additions wait out a timelock that depositors can watch. A longer delay gives you more time to assess a proposed change; a short timelock gives you less time to exit before it takes effect. Also account for the performance fee: a Curator can charge up to 50% of interest, so compare what remains after the fee.

Share-price history: An established vault has a share-price history through volatile periods, which gives you evidence of how its strategy behaved under stress. A new vault has no such history. That record cannot guarantee future results, but it lets you examine how the vault’s value changed before committing a large deposit.

Consider two new vaults with the same TVL, Curator, collateral, LLTV and timelock. In the first, the APY premium comes from temporary rewards; choose the established vault, because the premium can disappear when incentives end. In the second, the premium comes from native yield and the Curator is already one you would accept; a small test deposit is reasonable, while the established vault remains the core holding. Changing the yield source changes the decision; the other conditions stay fixed.

Choose the established lower-APY vault when the premium depends on rewards, riskier collateral or a higher LLTV, or when the new vault’s small TVL makes dilution material. Test the new high-APY vault with a small slice when its yield is native, the Curator is known, and the collateral, LLTV and timelock fit your limits. If you are unsure, split the deposit and keep most of it in the established vault.

Related

How long before a vault counts as established?

A vault counts as established when it has enough operating and share-price history for you to assess its behavior through volatile periods. Age alone is not the test; the record and the strategy behind it matter.

Why do new vaults launch with reward incentives?

New vaults can use incentives to attract deposits while their TVL is small. Treat those rewards as temporary yield and decide whether the vault still suits you after they end.

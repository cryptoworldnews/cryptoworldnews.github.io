How Should Beginners Choose an EigenLayer Operator?

A beginner checklist for choosing a restaking operator: compare its AVSs and Operator Sets, per-AVS commission, slashing and uptime history, and delegation access.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 20, 2026 · 04:30 UTC#697eb43 min read

A beginner with restaked ETH or stETH should choose an EigenLayer operator by the AVSs and Operator Sets receiving the stake, each per-AVS commission, and the operator’s slashing and uptime record, rather than its total delegated stake.

Delegation assigns your restaked assets to an operator, which allocates stake to AVSs, or actively validated services. Each AVS and Operator Set has its own slashing conditions, so the services receiving your stake shape your exposure. Commission affects rewards after the operator’s share is taken.

Before comparing operators, confirm your restaking position is complete and visible; the balance available to delegate is the basis for your choice. On eigen layer, the restaking step establishes that position. See how to restake ETH and verify an eigen layer position so you can confirm the balance you plan to delegate before comparing operators.

Run four checks before delegating

Use the operator’s eigen layer page to build a shortlist from its service mix, fees, record, and delegation settings.

  • AVS and Operator Set list: Write down every AVS and Operator Set to which the operator allocates your stake. Read the slashing conditions for each one: every allocation can expose your assets to a different set of penalties. A familiar operator name does not tell you which services it supports. Compare the list with the risks you are willing to take, and keep only operators whose service mix you understand.
  • Commission per AVS: Record the commission for each AVS separately. The default split is 10%, but an operator sets commission per AVS, so one headline fee cannot describe the cost of every allocation. The split can also change after a delay. Compare the current splits across the AVSs you care about, and account for the possibility that they may differ later. Your net rewards depend on the split for the AVS generating them, not on a single operator-wide rate.
  • Slashing and uptime record: Review the operator’s uptime history and any past slashing on its page, then check explorers for additional records. These are evidence about how the operator has performed; read them alongside the AVS list, since each service carries its own conditions. A record with no visible incident does not remove the risk attached to a current allocation. Keep a note of what you found for each shortlisted operator so the comparison is based on the same checks.
  • Delegation approver status: Confirm whether the operator accepts open delegation or has set a delegation approver. An approver must sign off on new delegations, so a technically complete position may still need approval before it can be delegated. Check the operator’s stated process and whether your delegation can proceed; use this to remove options you cannot access now.

Total delegated stake is a poor shortcut for this decision. Choosing the largest operator by TVL concentrates your stake with one operator and says nothing about its AVS mix, per-service commission, record, or approver settings. Treat size as context, then compare the four checks above.

Related questions

Can I delegate to more than one operator?

A staker delegates to one operator at a time. That means the shortlist helps you choose which operator receives your delegation; it is not a way to split the same position across several operators at once.

Where can I see which AVSs an operator runs?

Start on the operator’s page and note its AVSs and Operator Sets, then use explorers to check relevant performance and slashing records. With the list, commissions, record, and approver status in hand, you can compare operators on the exposures and net rewards tied to your own position.

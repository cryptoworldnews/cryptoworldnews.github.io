CFTC Adds Tokenized Asset and Blockchain Recordkeeping Guidance

The CFTC added FAQ guidance on tokenized permitted investments and blockchain recordkeeping, clarifying how registrants can apply existing rules to both.

The Crypto Bulletin DeskSep 24, 20262 min read

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission’s staff updated its crypto FAQs on September 24, adding guidance on investing customer funds in tokenized permitted assets and using blockchain to meet recordkeeping requirements. The update concerns how registrants work within existing rules; the agency’s announcement of the FAQ changes says it addresses those two operational areas.

What does it mean to invest in a tokenized permitted asset?

A permitted investment is an asset a futures commission merchant (FCM) or derivatives clearing organization (DCO) may invest customer funds in under applicable rules. Tokenization records a digital representation of an asset on a blockchain. The CFTC’s earlier guidance describes examples such as U.S. Treasury securities, corporate bonds and money market fund shares.

The token is a digital record tied to the underlying asset. The key question is whether the token gives its holder legal and economic rights that are the same as, or functionally equivalent to, holding that asset in traditional form. The CFTC’s Staff Letter 25-39 on tokenized collateral says the token’s form does not by itself change the asset’s basic characteristics, but each asset and tokenization structure must be assessed against applicable requirements.

Those requirements include legal enforceability, custody and segregation, and risk controls. For collateral, a clearing organization must also account for credit, market and liquidity risks when setting haircuts. The September FAQ update brings investments of customer funds in tokenized permitted investments into the agency’s published answers.

How can blockchain satisfy recordkeeping requirements?

The update also addresses when registrants may use blockchain technology to satisfy recordkeeping requirements. The CFTC announcement confirms the topic, but does not describe the updated answers’ specific conditions or technical tests. The change places blockchain recordkeeping alongside tokenized investments in the FAQs’ operational guidance for registrants and registered entities.

That distinction matters because the FAQ concerns how firms carry out existing regulatory duties using blockchain technology. The agency announced guidance on its use for recordkeeping, not a new recordkeeping rule.

Who issued the update, and what does it change?

The Market Participants Division, Division of Market Oversight and Division of Clearing and Risk issued the update. The FAQs were first released on March 20, 2026, to clarify staff letters on tokenized collateral and digital assets accepted as margin collateral. The revised topics extend that FAQ work from collateral handling to customer fund investments and recordkeeping.

The CFTC described the update as further clarity for market participants. It did not announce a change to the underlying rules in its release. Firms dealing with tokenized assets or blockchain records will need to read the updated answers alongside the requirements that apply to their specific activities.

EU proposes sanctions on Russian banks and crypto networks

The European Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions proposal would bar transactions with about 60 banks, target crypto networks and restrict Russian teams at EU sports events.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 27, 2026 · 09:15 UTC#35c77b3 min read

The European Commission has proposed extending its Russia sanctions to about 60 banks and crypto networks, adding measures that would also bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under their flag at EU sports events. The package was presented on 23 November and is not yet in force.

The proposal’s financial measures work through intermediaries. A transaction ban would restrict dealings with the listed banks, while removing those banks from SWIFT would cut their access to the messaging network banks use to coordinate international payments. For crypto, the Commission says it wants to crack down on networks in Russia and in third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. It has also launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that effort, according to European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen’s statement on the 22nd package.

What would the proposed crypto measures cover?

The Commission has not described the crypto proposal as a blanket ban on all Russian crypto activity. Its statement names crypto and payment networks, and says the measures would focus on Russia while also including third countries that assist sanctions evasion. It does not identify specific platforms in the statement. The task force is intended to help coordinate work on this part of the package.

That distinction matters: the proposal targets networks and services connected to sanctions circumvention, as described by the Commission, rather than setting out a general rule for every crypto user or transaction. The statement gives no list of affected crypto firms or technical criteria for how a network would be covered.

Who else could be affected by the package?

The bank measure covers around 60 Russian banks, which the Commission says would face a transaction ban and removal from SWIFT. The proposal also adds restrictions outside finance. The Commission says it would list more than 70 vessels linked to Russia’s shadow fleet and ban tankers insured in Russia from EU ports.

In sports, Russian teams and athletes would be barred from competing under the Russian flag at events in the EU. The proposal would also prohibit official Russian symbols at those events, restrict Russian sponsors’ financial flows to European sports and cultural associations, and restrict Russians from holding governing posts in European sports federations.

When could the measures take effect?

The package remains a proposal. The Associated Press report on the Commission’s plan says all 27 EU member countries must endorse it before it can enter into force; the process can take weeks, and the measures may change. Until approval, the proposed bank and crypto restrictions are not new rules in force.

For crypto businesses, the immediate development is the Commission’s stated intent to target evasion routes and coordinate enforcement through its new task force. The final scope will depend on the package approved by EU governments.

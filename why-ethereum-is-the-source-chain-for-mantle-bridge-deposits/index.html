Why Ethereum Is the Source Chain for Mantle Bridge Deposits

For a canonical Mantle transfer, Ethereum is the source chain; choose it when the asset, settlement path, timing and fee budget fit your business controls.

Merkle Street NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 09:19 UTC#4f75bf4 min read

For a business using Mantle’s canonical bridge, Ethereum is the source chain for deposits into Mantle. The bridge connects Ethereum and Mantle; it does not let a business pick any network as the source. First, the sender selects a supported token and enters a destination address. If the token needs approval, the sender authorises the bridge contract to use it. The sender then submits the deposit transaction on Ethereum and pays that network’s fee in ETH.

After Ethereum confirms the deposit, the bridge’s message is processed on Mantle and the corresponding supported token becomes available there. The sending wallet, receiving address and token contract all matter: the same ticker can refer to different tokens on different networks. A guide to a first Mantle bridge transfer covers the transaction sequence in more detail. For a business, the choice starts with whether Ethereum holds the asset and whether the deposit path fits its settlement and accounting needs.

Which source chain does the Mantle bridge use?

For a canonical deposit, the source chain is Ethereum. The canonical route is built to move supported assets between Ethereum and Mantle, so a business holding funds on another network cannot simply select that network in the source field. It must first get the asset onto Ethereum through a route it has assessed, or use a different transfer path.

That distinction affects both cost and control. A transfer from another network can involve more than one transaction and more than one service. The business should account for each step, including any conversion, bridge fee, settlement delay and reconciliation record. A route that looks cheaper at the first step may cost more across the full journey.

What happens to the asset during a deposit?

The bridge contract receives the deposit on Ethereum and the bridge process makes the corresponding asset available on Mantle. This is a transfer between networks, not automatically a swap into another token. The destination token may have a different contract address from its Ethereum counterpart, even when both display the same ticker.

That is why the token mapping matters. Before sending, confirm that the bridge supports the asset in the selected direction and that the version arriving on Mantle is accepted by the business’s wallet, application or treasury process. The receiving address must also be usable on Mantle. Keep a record of the Ethereum transaction and check the destination balance after the bridge processes it.

Deposits and withdrawals also have different steps. A deposit begins on Ethereum and ends with the asset available on Mantle. A withdrawal begins on Mantle and must complete the bridge’s settlement process before the asset is available on Ethereum. A business planning cash movement should allow for the route’s actual settlement process rather than assume both directions take the same time.

How should a business decide when funds are elsewhere?

Start with the asset’s current location and the required destination. If the funds are already on Ethereum and the business needs them on Mantle, the canonical bridge is the direct route to assess. If funds are elsewhere, compare the whole route to Ethereum plus the Mantle deposit, or assess a separate route that supports the required source and destination.

  • Check that the exact token and direction are supported.
  • Estimate fees for approvals, transfers and any conversion along the route.
  • Confirm the destination wallet or application accepts the Mantle token version.
  • Set an internal process for recording transaction IDs and verifying receipt.

Ethereum is therefore the practical source chain for a canonical Mantle deposit, provided the business holds the right asset there and can meet the fee and settlement requirements. When it does not, the source-chain decision is really a route decision: compare the complete path, token compatibility and operational controls before moving funds.

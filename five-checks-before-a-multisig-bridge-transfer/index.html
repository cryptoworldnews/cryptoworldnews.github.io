Five Checks Before a Multisig Bridge Transfer

A multisig bridge transfer passes through source finality, signer approval and destination execution; five checks show where to verify each step before acting.

Merkle Street NewsroomOct 20, 2026 · 14:06 UTC#7e45874 min read

A multisig bridge transfer moves an asset or message from one blockchain to another after a group of authorized signers approves the source transaction. The source contract records or locks the asset, validators observe the event, and enough signers approve a message for the destination contract to release or mint funds. Before sending, check the route, contracts, transfer details, signer progress and destination result.

How does a multisig bridge transfer work?

The source chain first processes the user's transaction. Depending on the bridge design, a contract locks the asset or burns it, then emits an event containing details such as the token, amount and destination address. Validators watch the source chain and report that event. A multisig wallet or contract collects their signatures until the required threshold is met.

Once approved, a relayer can submit the signed message to the destination chain. The destination contract checks the signatures and message, then releases previously locked tokens or mints a representation of them. Think of the signatures as several keys needed to open one lock: the bridge acts only after enough authorized keys have approved the same transfer. For a fuller account of how route choice and delays fit together, see mayan bridge; here, the key distinction is between source confirmation and destination execution.

These are separate steps. A confirmed source transaction does not prove that the destination contract has acted. A transfer can wait for the source chain to reach the bridge's required finality, for signers to approve the message, or for someone to submit it on the destination chain. The bridge interface may show these stages under different status labels.

What should you check before sending?

Check the transfer details before you authorize the source transaction. A bridge transfer may be irreversible once submitted, and a correct token name alone does not prove that you selected the intended route or destination asset.

  • Check the route and token. Confirm the source chain, destination chain and asset on both sides. A token with the same ticker can have a different contract address or be a wrapped representation.
  • Check the contracts. Verify that the source bridge contract and destination bridge contract match the route you intend to use. A wallet prompt should agree with the action shown in the bridge interface.
  • Check the recipient and amount. Review the destination address, transfer amount, fee and any minimum amount the recipient must receive. Make sure the address belongs to the intended destination chain.
  • Check the transfer limits. Read the displayed minimum, maximum and any route-specific restrictions before signing. If the amount is outside the stated limits, the transfer may fail or need support to resolve.

Fifth, check progress after sending. Save the source transaction hash and use it to follow the transfer. Confirm the source transaction succeeded, then look for the bridge's signer or approval status. After approval, check for a destination transaction and verify that the destination wallet received the expected asset. A pending status can mean the message is waiting for a later step; it does not by itself mean the funds are lost.

What does a pending transfer mean?

A pending transfer means at least one step has not been completed, but the status alone does not identify which one. Start with the source transaction hash. If the source transaction failed, the bridge cannot continue. If it succeeded, check whether the source chain has reached the bridge's finality requirement and whether the multisig threshold has approved the message.

If approval is complete but no destination transaction appears, the message may still need to be submitted or processed on the destination chain. Use the bridge's own transfer record to check for a destination transaction hash, then verify that transaction on the destination chain. This separates a slow or incomplete relay from a completed transfer that is simply not visible in the wallet yet.

Contact support only through a channel linked from the bridge's verified interface if the displayed state conflicts with the source or destination transaction. Never share a seed phrase or private key to resolve a transfer; bridge support does not need either to inspect a public transaction. The practical rule is to treat each stage as its own check: source success, signer approval and destination execution.

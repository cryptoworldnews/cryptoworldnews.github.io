US Wins Court Authority Over $1.2B in Binance Theft Funds

US authorities say they froze $2.7 billion from the Binance theft and won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion as tracing continues.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 13:44 UTC#3c76363 min read

US authorities have frozen more than $2.7 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance and obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of it, the Justice Department said on November 12. The department says investigators and private-sector partners worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the funds. That means the exchanges and other services handling assets were asked to help stop them moving beyond law enforcement’s reach, according to the Justice Department announcement.

The order covers only part of the funds authorities say have been frozen. It does not mean the government has taken ownership of all $1.2 billion or recovered the entire amount stolen. The Associated Press reports that a judge would determine who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds through a forfeiture process.

From the Binance theft to a court order

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. The Justice Department says the theft happened on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets taken from Binance. The AP describes the theft as about $6.3 billion and reports that the FBI has called it the largest crypto heist in history.

After the theft, agencies including the Justice Department’s National Security and Criminal divisions, the US Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia and law enforcement partners worked with virtual asset service providers. Their combined efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion, the department said. A freeze blocks the movement of assets through cooperating services; court authorization gives the government legal power to seize or restrain a portion of those frozen funds.

The distinction matters because tracing and freezing can happen before a final decision about who gets the assets. A freeze can keep cryptocurrency from moving while investigators track it. The court process can then determine whether it can be forfeited to the government and returned to people with a valid claim.

Tracing continues across crypto services

The Justice Department said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder the stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchain networks. Mixers pool and redistribute transactions to obscure where funds came from. Exchanges can convert or transfer crypto. Investigators are working to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit assets moving through those routes, the department said.

Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert urging virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used by TraderTraitor, including transactions derived from those addresses. The FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit are coordinating the recovery effort with the other agencies.

The AP reports that the next step in a typical recovery is for the government to file a forfeiture claim with a judge. The judge then considers who has a legitimate claim to the recovered funds. The Justice Department says its tracing and seizure work is ongoing, so the court-authorized amount is a step in the recovery process, not a final accounting of who will receive the assets.

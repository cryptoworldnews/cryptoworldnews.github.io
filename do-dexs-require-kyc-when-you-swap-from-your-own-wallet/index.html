Do DEXs Require KYC When You Swap From Your Own Wallet?

Do decentralized exchanges require KYC? Usually no: a wallet-signed swap needs no account, while fiat on-ramps and centralized exchanges may ask for ID.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 24, 2026 · 22:57 UTC#4fd6724 min read

Decentralized exchanges usually do not require KYC for a swap: you connect a self-custody wallet and sign a transaction, while identity checks may apply at the fiat and centralized-exchange services around it.

How does a DEX swap work?

A DEX swap is a transaction your wallet signs and sends to a smart contract, with no exchange account to verify.

Your wallet holds the tokens and asks you to approve or submit transactions. The smart contract processes the swap using the terms and liquidity available to it. Your wallet is the key that authorizes the transaction; the contract is the part that carries it out.

An aggregator searches routes across supported liquidity sources, then presents a route for your wallet to execute. KyberSwap is a decentralized exchange aggregator: it helps you find routes for swapping tokens across supported liquidity sources and networks. Using it means connecting a self-custody wallet and having the chain’s native coin to pay gas. There is no account or ID upload for the swap.

Does KyberSwap ask for ID?

KyberSwap’s swap flow uses a connected self-custody wallet, so the swap itself does not call for an identity document.

That answers the usual question for someone who has swapped before: you use your wallet to authorize an on-chain transaction, rather than signing into a trading account with your name and personal details. A wallet connection lets a site interact with the wallet; signing a transaction authorizes a specific action on-chain.

A front-end can still screen access by location or check wallet addresses against sanctions lists such as OFAC’s Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list. That is a separate access check. A site can restrict a wallet or region without asking the user to complete KYC.

Where does KYC enter a DeFi trade?

KYC usually enters when money or crypto passes through a business that identifies its customers, such as a fiat on-ramp or centralized exchange.

To buy crypto with a bank card, cash out to a bank account, or trade on a centralized exchange, you may need to provide identity documents. Those services handle the link between your identity and the account or payment method. Once crypto is in your self-custody wallet, a DEX swap can happen through a smart contract without opening another account.

But no KYC does not mean anonymity. Blockchains record transactions publicly, and explorers such as Etherscan let anyone inspect activity by address. The chain shows addresses and transactions, not a person’s name. The connection can come from outside the chain: say you withdraw from a KYC’d centralized exchange to your wallet. That withdrawal ties the address to your exchange identity, and later swaps from that address can be followed on-chain. Further transfers can create more links to trace.

KyberSwap can help find a route across supported liquidity sources, but the transaction still comes from your wallet and appears on-chain. The kyberswap elastic name refers to a KyberSwap product; the key privacy point is that public transactions remain visible whichever route you use.

Before swapping, decide whether you are comfortable using a publicly traceable wallet, and check whether the service’s access rules apply to your location or address.

Questions readers ask next

Can a DEX front-end block my wallet address?

Yes. A front-end may restrict access based on location or sanctions screening, including checks against OFAC’s SDN list. That screening can happen without requesting KYC.

Do I need to report DEX swaps for taxes?

Tax reporting depends on the rules where you live and the details of your transactions. Keep records of swaps and consult your local tax authority or a qualified tax professional.

Which wallet should I use to trade on KyberSwap?

Use a self-custody wallet that supports the network you plan to trade on and that you can secure and manage. Keep enough of the network’s native coin in it to pay gas.

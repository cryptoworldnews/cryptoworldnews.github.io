Native Gas Tokens and Cross-Chain Fees

A native gas token in crypto is the coin a blockchain uses for transaction fees; learn which coin each network needs before you send or bridge assets.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 07:33 UTC#a895e33 min read

A blockchain charges each transaction in its required gas coin, and that payment is spent as the transaction runs. So what is a native gas token in crypto, and which coin should you hold before sending or bridging your tokens?

A native gas token is the coin a blockchain requires for transaction fees. Each transaction spends some of it to be processed: validators or sequencers accept fees in that coin. Think of it as the fare for using a particular network. Owning the asset you want to send does not automatically cover that fare.

Which coin pays gas on each network?

Ethereum, Arbitrum, Optimism and Base all charge gas in ETH. Polygon PoS charges gas in POL, which replaced MATIC in 2024. The coin you need depends on the network where you are making the transaction.

Take a USDC transfer on Base. USDC is the asset being sent, but the transaction still costs a small amount of ETH. The wallet needs enough ETH on Base to pay that fee. ETH on Ethereum is on a different network, so having ETH there does not pay a Base transaction fee.

Arbitrum and Optimism make a useful distinction clear: their branded tokens, ARB and OP, are governance tokens. They are used for governance, while gas on those networks is paid in ETH. Holding ARB on Arbitrum or OP on Optimism does not cover a transaction fee. Treating a network’s branded token as its gas token can leave you with tokens to send but no way to pay for the transaction.

For a cross-chain transfer, the source chain is where your transaction starts. In Jumper bridge, you pay gas on that source chain in its native token before the transfer begins. The Jumper bridge routing process adds context on how supported bridges and swaps can form a route between networks. Jumper bridge can help find that route, but you still need the source chain’s gas coin to start.

That means the fee coin follows the network, not the token being moved or the brand on the network. Before sending from Arbitrum, have ETH on Arbitrum. Before sending from Polygon PoS, have POL there. Before sending USDC from Base, have ETH on Base.

Can I pay gas fees with USDC?

Usually, a transaction requires the network’s gas coin even when the asset being transferred is USDC. On Base, for instance, the USDC transfer needs ETH for gas. Keep some of the required coin on the same network as the transaction.

How much gas token should I keep in my wallet?

There is no single amount that fits every wallet. The fee depends on the transaction and network conditions, so a simple transfer and a more involved transaction can cost different amounts. Check the wallet’s fee estimate before you confirm, then leave enough of the network’s gas coin for the action you plan to take.

Before you send or bridge, confirm the source network and its gas coin in your wallet. That check helps ensure you can pay the fee and start the transfer.

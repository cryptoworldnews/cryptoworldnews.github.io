What a Bridge Upgrade Notice Means for Long-Term Holdings

A bridge upgrade notice can change where a transfer is sent or how it is verified; long-term holders should check the route, deadline and withdrawal path.

Merkle Street NewsroomSep 29, 2026#c0a4a53 min read

A bridge upgrade notice tells holders that the software or contracts moving assets between networks are changing, and it may require action before a route closes. A bridge usually has a contract on the source network that locks or burns an asset, a contract on the destination network that releases or creates a representation, and validators or other operators that confirm the transfer. The notice identifies which part is changing and whether existing deposits, withdrawals or balances are affected.

What changes during a bridge upgrade?

An upgrade can replace a contract, change the route between networks, or update the software that verifies transfers. Operators may pause deposits and withdrawals while they install and test the change. Some upgrades also move activity to a new contract or route, while others leave existing balances where they are and affect only future transfers. The notice should say which case applies.

Think of a bridge as a guarded doorway between two networks: an upgrade can change the lock, the guard’s checks or the doorway itself. For route-specific detail, a fuller manta bridge explainer can help clarify what a route refers to. The general rule is to match the notice to the exact network pair, asset and bridge contract you use.

A token held on the destination network may be a representation issued by the bridge, rather than the original asset. If a notice retires that representation or its route, holders may need to return it through the bridge or use a stated migration process. If the notice says balances are unaffected, there may be no holder action at all. Read the scope before moving funds.

How can holders tell whether they need to act?

Start by checking whether the notice names your asset, source network and destination network. Then look for a deadline, a pause window, a replacement contract address and instructions for balances already on the destination network. A change to future transfers does not automatically mean that a long-term balance must move. The notice needs to explain how existing holdings are handled.

For a long-term holding, the useful details are:

  • Which deposits, withdrawals or balances the upgrade affects.
  • Whether the current route will stop, and when.
  • Whether holders must migrate, redeem or approve a new contract.
  • How to confirm the official instructions and contract address.

If the notice is unclear about an existing balance, do not infer that a transfer is required just because a new route is available. Check the project’s official channels and the bridge interface reached through a trusted bookmark. A message asking for a seed phrase or a wallet signature unrelated to the stated migration is not a normal upgrade step.

What is the safer choice for a holder?

For most long-term holders, the better choice is to leave an unaffected balance alone and act only when the notice clearly says that balance needs migration or redemption. This avoids paying fees or exposing funds to an unfamiliar route without a stated need. If action is required, verify the network and destination, test with a small transfer when the instructions allow it, and keep a record of the transaction.

A bridge upgrade changes the machinery that carries assets between networks; it does not, by itself, tell you that every token already held has moved or become invalid. The decision turns on the notice’s scope: identify the route, find the treatment of existing balances, and follow the stated steps only if your holding is included.

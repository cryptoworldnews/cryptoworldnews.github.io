ESMA sets Jan. 8 deadline for EU crypto stablecoin holdings

ESMA told national regulators to stop EU crypto services for non-MiCA stablecoins, giving platforms up to three months to resolve customer holdings.

Merkle Street NewsroomOct 8, 2026 · 23:53 UTC#c33c573 min read

The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) told national regulators to stop EU-authorised crypto firms serving customers with stablecoins that fail to meet the bloc’s MiCA rules, setting a three-month limit to resolve existing holdings. In an opinion published on Oct. 8, ESMA said providers should not maintain or enable access to those tokens. Any remaining customer holdings must be addressed as soon as possible and no later than three months after publication — Jan. 8, 2027.

Which services must crypto firms stop?

The restriction applies to crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) authorised under MiCA, and covers more than listing a token on a trading platform. ESMA’s opinion includes exchanges, order execution, transfers, custody and administration, investment advice and portfolio management. The tokens in question are asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens, the two MiCA categories ESMA uses for stablecoins.

National competent authorities, the regulators in each EU member state, are to supervise firms’ compliance. They should ensure providers do not maintain, introduce or facilitate customer access to non-compliant tokens. The controls may be technical, contractual or organisational, and should prevent customers from acquiring more or increasing their exposure.

In practice, the rule closes the route for new purchases and trading through authorised platforms, then gives regulators a deadline for resolving balances that were already there. It works like closing a shop to new sales while allowing a short, supervised window to clear existing orders.

What can customers do with tokens they already hold?

During the wind-down, firms may continue only services needed to liquidate, convert, withdraw, transfer or safeguard existing tokens. ESMA says those services must be time-limited, risk-based and closely supervised. The opinion does not prescribe one process for every platform; national regulators oversee how firms handle their remaining customer exposures.

That distinction matters for a customer holding a non-compliant stablecoin on an exchange. The platform may allow a sale, conversion or withdrawal while it winds down the holding, but it cannot use the transition period to keep the token available for ordinary trading or to let customers build a larger position.

Which stablecoins could be affected?

ESMA’s opinion does not name tokens. CoinDesk’s report on the guidance identifies Tether’s USDT as a prominent example of a stablecoin that is not authorised under MiCA. The rule applies to any asset-referenced or e-money token that does not meet the regulation’s requirements, rather than to a named coin list.

MiCA sets requirements for stablecoin issuers, including authorisation, reserves, redemption and disclosures, according to CoinDesk. ESMA’s position links those issuer rules to the services platforms provide: authorised firms must not let EU customers access non-compliant tokens through trading, custody or other covered services. National regulators now have up to three months to ensure any pre-existing customer holdings are resolved.

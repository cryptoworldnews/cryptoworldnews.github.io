Different Wallet Keys Do Not Stop a Crypto Bridge

You can bridge crypto to a wallet with different keys: the source wallet signs the deposit, while the bridge sends the destination asset to your chosen address.

Merkle Street NewsroomOct 17, 2026 · 10:06 UTC#09ab604 min read

You can bridge crypto between wallets with different keys by signing the deposit with the source wallet and entering the destination wallet’s public address. The bridge does not need the two wallets to share a recovery phrase or private key. It needs a supported route between the networks and an address that can receive the destination asset.

First, the source wallet signs a transaction that sends tokens to a bridge contract or other bridge-controlled address. The bridge then verifies that deposit, often by waiting for the source chain to confirm it. Depending on its design, it may lock the original tokens and release or mint a corresponding asset on the destination chain, or arrange a swap between assets. A closer look at Biswap and the mechanics of moving Bitcoin to Ethereum explains how a route can involve different networks and asset forms. In the bridge form, you select the destination network and provide the receiving address. The destination wallet’s key is used later to control the funds, not to authorize the source deposit.

Do both wallets need the same keys?

No. A bridge transfer can start from one wallet and end at an address controlled by another wallet. The source wallet signs the transaction that spends or deposits the funds. The bridge uses its verification process to determine whether the destination transfer should proceed. The receiving wallet does not sign the source transaction.

A wallet is software or hardware that manages keys; the address is where a network records assets. Two wallets can have different keys and different addresses. Some wallet setups can show the same address on several compatible networks, but that does not mean all chains share addresses or assets. Bitcoin and Ethereum, for example, use different address formats. Check the destination network and copy the receiving address from the wallet set up to control funds on that network.

What happens after you send the deposit?

The bridge processes the transfer in stages. The source transaction is broadcast and confirmed by validators on its network. The bridge or its validators then check that the deposit is valid and that the requested destination and amount match the transfer. After that, the destination network processes a release, mint, or swap, and its validators record the resulting transaction. The destination wallet can display the asset once that transaction is confirmed and the wallet supports the token.

The analogy is a parcel service: you hand a parcel to one counter and name a different recipient. The recipient does not need your account password, but the address must be deliverable. A blockchain bridge has the extra job of checking events on one network and triggering an action on another. That connection adds a separate mechanism and potential failure point to an ordinary transfer.

What should you check before bridging?

Check the route, token, and destination address before signing. A token with the same name can exist on multiple networks without being the same asset. The bridge’s destination selection must match the network your receiving wallet can access, and the address must belong to an account or wallet you control there. Also check the amount delivered after fees, the required source gas, and whether the bridge shows a minimum deposit or estimated processing time.

  • Confirm the source and destination networks, including the exact token on each.
  • Copy the receiving address from the destination wallet and verify its format.
  • Review the transaction in the source wallet before signing; it should match the transfer you intend.
  • Keep enough of the destination network’s native token for any later transaction that needs a network fee.

If the source and destination are the same network, a direct wallet transfer is usually simpler than a bridge. If they are different networks, separate keys are fine: the essential questions are whether the bridge supports that route and whether you can control the destination address. Never enter a recovery phrase or private key into a bridge form. A bridge needs authorization for the source transaction, not the secret that controls your receiving wallet.

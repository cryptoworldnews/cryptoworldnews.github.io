US wins court authority over $1.2bn in Binance hack crypto

US officials have frozen over $2.7 billion from the Binance theft and won court authority over $1.2 billion, with tracing and forfeiture work still underway.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 17, 2026 · 10:48 UTC#414a1a3 min read

When cryptocurrency moves through a wallet, a bridge or an exchange, investigators can trace its path and ask service providers to block it. The US Justice Department says it has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, within a larger pool of more than $2.7 billion that government and private-sector partners have frozen.

The department announced the action on Nov. 12. Its announcement says the theft happened on or around Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The FBI attributes it to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. The Associated Press reported the seizure announcement and said the theft was about $6.3 billion.

The numbers describe different steps. A freeze blocks funds from moving, often through cooperation with virtual asset service providers. A court order authorizes the government to seize or restrain specified assets. The Justice Department says more than $2.7 billion has been frozen so far; over $1.2 billion of that amount has court authorization for seizure or restraint. That is not the same as a final decision on who owns the funds.

Tracing continues as funds move through crypto services

The department says investigators and service providers are still tracing the stolen assets as TraderTraitor actors move them through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. A bridge moves tokens between blockchains. A mixer is designed to make transaction paths harder to follow. An exchange can provide another point where funds may be identified and blocked.

The process resembles stopping a package at several transfer depots: each service that handles the assets may be able to halt the next leg, while investigators build a record of where the funds went. In crypto, the public ledger can show transfers between addresses, but control of an address does not by itself identify the person behind it.

The FBI had issued a cyber alert the previous week, according to the Justice Department, attributing the Binance theft and urging virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the stolen funds. The department says the government and private firms worked together to freeze funds before they moved beyond law enforcement’s reach.

The next confirmed steps are continued tracing, seizure and forfeiture efforts. The Justice Department says those efforts are ongoing. The AP reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets. The department’s announcement does not say that the full $1.2 billion has been returned to Binance or its customers, or set a date for a final ownership decision.

US wins authority over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department won authority to seize or restrain $1.2 billion of Binance theft proceeds, as tracing and recovery efforts continue across crypto services.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 18, 2026 · 09:46 UTC#ecb42d3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while efforts to trace and recover the funds continue across crypto services. The department announced the action on Nov. 12, saying the assets are part of a larger pool frozen by U.S. agencies and private companies.

Here is how the recovery works. Investigators trace the stolen assets as they move between blockchain addresses. When funds reach a virtual asset service provider, or VASP, such as an exchange, that provider can help interdict them. The Justice Department says law enforcement has worked with VASPs to stop the assets moving beyond its reach. That is similar to placing a hold on funds at an intermediary, though the crypto may have moved through several wallets and services first.

The court authorization gives the government legal power to seize or restrain the identified assets. It does not mean a court has already decided who should ultimately receive them. The Justice Department announcement says the government and private sector have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total since the Binance theft, with more than $1.2 billion of that amount covered by the court authorization.

Tracing continues as funds move through crypto services

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. The department says the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets taken from Binance. The Associated Press report describes the theft as about $6.3 billion and notes that the FBI had previously attributed it to the group.

Authorities say the recovery work is still under way. The Justice Department reports that TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. A bridge moves tokens between blockchains; mixers can obscure transaction links; exchanges can convert or hold assets. Investigators are working with the FBI, the U.S. Secret Service and the IRS Criminal Investigation division to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the funds.

The next confirmed legal step is forfeiture. In such cases, the government typically files a claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets, the AP reported. The Justice Department has not said when that process will conclude or how much of the frozen total will be returned. For now, the $1.2 billion figure describes assets covered by court authority to seize or restrain them, within a broader recovery effort that has frozen more than $2.7 billion.

US wins court authority over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department says a court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2bn in crypto stolen from Binance as tracing and forfeiture efforts continue.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 21, 2026 · 23:28 UTC#58488a2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The order covers part of a larger pool that government agencies and private-sector firms have frozen while they trace the funds.

The department’s November 12 announcement describes two steps: virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, help freeze assets, then the government obtains court authority to seize or restrain some of them. A freeze blocks the funds from moving while the legal process continues. It works like a hold on an account, though the assets remain cryptocurrency.

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. The Justice Department says the theft happened on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The Associated Press reported the theft at about $6.3 billion and said the FBI had previously linked the group to North Korea.

More than $2.7 billion frozen so far

According to the Justice Department, coordinated U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets. The department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that total. The figures describe overlapping stages of the recovery effort, not separate sums to add together.

The distinction matters: a freeze can stop funds from being transferred, while a seizure or restraint backed by a court order gives the government legal control to pursue forfeiture. The AP reports that in such cases, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim to the recovered funds.

Tracing continues across crypto services

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets remain ongoing. It says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchain networks; mixers can make transaction trails harder to follow; exchanges can convert or hold digital assets.

Last week, the FBI, U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert attributing the theft and urging VASPs to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses the group is using to launder the stolen Binance assets. That makes the next step a continuing race between investigators tracing funds across services and the hackers moving them. The Justice Department has not said that all frozen funds have been forfeited or returned.

US targets $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance

The Justice Department says court orders cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack funds, part of $2.7 billion frozen as tracing and recovery continue.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 14:44 UTC#382eb52 min read

The US Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, part of a larger pool of funds frozen after the October hack. The department announced the action on November 12, saying tracing and recovery efforts are still underway.

The funds moved out of Binance on October 29, when North Korean military hackers identified by the FBI as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets, according to the Justice Department’s announcement. Investigators and private-sector partners then tracked the stolen assets and worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to stop some from moving further.

That response depends on where the stolen cryptocurrency goes. Blockchain records show transfers between addresses. When investigators identify addresses linked to the theft, exchanges and other VASPs can be asked to block transactions involving those funds. The FBI issued an alert the week before the announcement urging providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses TraderTraitor was using, the Justice Department said.

Some stolen assets can pass through cryptocurrency bridges, which move assets between blockchains, or through mixers and exchanges. Those services can complicate efforts to follow the trail. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through these routes, while authorities and partners keep tracing them.

A freeze and a seizure are different steps. A freeze blocks assets from moving; a seizure is a legal step to take control of them. Court authorization to seize or restrain the funds does not by itself mean the government has completed a final transfer or returned the money to Binance or its customers. It is more like placing assets under a legal hold while a court process determines what happens next.

The Justice Department says government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total. The more than $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is part of that amount, not an additional sum. The department says the work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets continues.

The next confirmed step is continued tracing and legal recovery. In such cases, the US government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds, the Associated Press reported. The Justice Department’s announcement did not say that this process had concluded.

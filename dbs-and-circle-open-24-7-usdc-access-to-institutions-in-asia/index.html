DBS and Circle open 24/7 USDC access to institutions in Asia

DBS will let eligible institutions mint and redeem Circle’s USDC around the clock, creating a bank route into stablecoin settlement across Asia.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 21, 2026 · 01:55 UTC#2715b72 min read

DBS will let eligible institutional clients mint and redeem Circle’s USDC through the Singapore bank, with near-instant settlement between US dollars and USDC available 24 hours a day. The firms announced the partnership on Nov. 18.

Here is the route: an institution uses DBS to request USDC issuance or redemption, and the associated USD-USDC settlement can take place through the bank at any time. Circle says its regulated affiliates issue USDC. The DBS announcement says direct access is intended to reduce institutions’ reliance on secondary-market liquidity and support treasury, settlement and liquidity management.

That gives eligible clients a bank channel to obtain or redeem the stablecoin instead of depending entirely on crypto exchanges and other secondary markets. Think of DBS as a staffed gateway between a bank account and USDC: the analogy stops at access, because the announcement does not specify the transaction steps, fees or eligibility rules.

Tokenised deposits offer a different route

The partnership lands as banks explore blockchain-based payment infrastructure of their own. CoinDesk reported that the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and Swift are exploring connections between their payment infrastructure, with HSBC, Standard Chartered and Bank of China (Hong Kong) looking at international transfers using tokenised bank deposits. Those deposits represent money held at commercial banks and recorded on a blockchain; they are distinct from stablecoins issued by companies.

DBS is pursuing both models. According to CoinDesk’s report, the bank is also connecting its tokenised deposit network with JPMorgan’s so eligible clients can transfer tokenised U.S. dollars between Singapore and the United States, including on weekends.

The difference is where the money sits and who issues the digital representation. USDC is issued by Circle’s regulated affiliates; a tokenised deposit represents a bank liability. DBS’s arrangement gives institutions access to USDC through a bank while the bank also develops deposit-based rails. The announcement does not say that the USDC service is already live, name the eligible clients or set a launch date.

DBS cited its research showing that 88% of surveyed large corporations were deploying or piloting tokenised-money use cases, while more than three in five were adopting or planning to adopt stablecoins. The figures point to institutional interest in both forms of digital money. The deal gives some of those institutions another way to reach USDC, with settlement outside banking hours.

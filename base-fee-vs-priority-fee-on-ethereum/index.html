Base Fee vs Priority Fee on Ethereum

Base fee is set by Ethereum and burned; priority fee is your tip to the validator. Learn how maxFeePerGas caps what you pay without being the charge.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 05:18 UTC#342ae54 min read

On Ethereum, the base fee is set by the network and burned, while the priority fee is a tip you choose that goes to the validator. EIP-1559 split the fee this way so the protocol prices block space and users can bid for faster inclusion.

How do the base fee and priority fee differ?

The base fee is the protocol’s price for using space in an Ethereum block. The network adjusts it automatically according to demand: it can rise or fall by up to 12.5% per block, depending on whether the previous block was above or below its gas target. EIP-1559 introduced this system with Ethereum’s London upgrade in August 2021.

The priority fee is the extra amount a user offers the validator to include a transaction. You can raise or lower this tip in your wallet. A higher tip can make a transaction more attractive for inclusion, while a small tip may be enough when the network is not busy.

The two fees differ in who sets them, where they go and how they change. The protocol sets the base fee, which is burned; demand for block space moves it up or down. You set the priority fee, which goes to the validator; your chosen tip can change independently of the base fee.

Consider approving USDC before using a USDC bridge on Ethereum. A USDC bridge moves USDC from one blockchain to another. In a busy block, the base fee can make up most of the transaction cost, while a small priority fee may be enough to get the approval included. Your wallet may also show maxFeePerGas. This is the most you are willing to pay per unit of gas, not the amount automatically charged. The transaction’s actual per-gas charge is the base fee plus the tip that fits under your maxFeePerGas. If the base fee rises, a higher maximum gives the transaction room to remain eligible. If the base fee and tip total less than your maximum, the unused gap is never charged.

Think of maxFeePerGas as a spending ceiling, not a bid that is paid in full. For instance, if your ceiling is above the base fee, the remaining room can cover the tip and protect against a base-fee increase before inclusion. If the ceiling is too low to cover the current base fee, the transaction cannot be included at that fee level.

Choose the base fee as your baseline when estimating what network demand will cost. Choose a higher priority fee when you want to offer the validator more for quicker inclusion. In either case, check maxFeePerGas as the per-gas ceiling: it limits the charge, while the base fee and effective tip determine what you actually pay.

What do readers ask next about Ethereum fees?

How much priority fee is enough to get included quickly?

There is no fixed tip that guarantees quick inclusion. The right amount depends on how much other users are offering and how quickly you want the transaction included. Your wallet’s suggested tip can be a starting point; raising it offers the validator more, subject to your maxFeePerGas ceiling.

Do layer 2 networks charge a base fee too?

Layer 2 networks can charge transaction fees, but their fee rules depend on the network. Ethereum’s base-fee mechanism and the way another network presents its charges are not automatically identical, so check that network’s wallet fee breakdown.

Why did my transaction cost less than the maximum shown?

Because maxFeePerGas is a ceiling, not a flat charge. Your actual per-gas fee is the base fee plus the effective priority fee, and any unused room below the maximum is not charged.

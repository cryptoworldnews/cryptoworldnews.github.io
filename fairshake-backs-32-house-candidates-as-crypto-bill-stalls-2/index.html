Fairshake backs 32 House candidates as crypto bill stalls

Fairshake named 32 House candidates for midterm support, including six slated for $1 million apiece, as the crypto market-structure bill remains stalled in the Senate.

Merkle Street NewsroomOct 7, 2026 · 07:59 UTC#7ae8653 min read

Fairshake, a major crypto political group, announced on Oct. 5 that it would support 32 House candidates in November’s midterm elections, including six slated for $1 million in spending each. The group’s plan shifts attention to the races that will decide who writes crypto rules in the next Congress, after a market-structure bill stalled in the Senate. The Associated Press reported the slate, which includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats.

How does Fairshake’s election spending work?

Fairshake’s spending is political activity funded by the group, rather than money paid directly into a candidate’s campaign account. The Federal Election Commission says Super PACs can raise unlimited contributions to fund independent political activity, but cannot contribute directly to federal candidates or coordinate spending with their campaigns. Think of it as a separate megaphone: the group can pay to make its own case to voters. The announcement does not specify how Fairshake will spend on each of the 32 races.

Six lawmakers are slated for $1 million apiece: Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, and Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. Roll Call reported the six names and the wider slate. Fairshake did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates.

Why are these candidates on the slate?

All 32 supported the crypto industry’s priority market-structure bill, according to AP. The bill would establish a federal regulatory framework for digital assets. The House passed it in July 2025, but the Senate blocked it in September 2026 after lawmakers failed to agree on ethics safeguards related to President Donald Trump’s crypto investments.

That link between votes and spending is central to Fairshake’s stated approach. Spokesperson Geoff Vetter told AP that the group backs pro-crypto candidates in both parties. The slate includes lawmakers in competitive reelection races, where outside spending could help candidates reach voters.

How much could Fairshake spend before November?

The six announced commitments total $6 million, but that is only the disclosed amount for those lawmakers. AP reported that Fairshake and affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash at the end of August. The group has not said whether it will spend more in House or Senate races.

Its Senate spending offers a measure of the scale. In September, Fairshake announced plans to spend nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. The House slate is broader across party lines, but the total budget for those 32 races remains unknown. For now, the confirmed plan is six $1 million commitments and support for 26 other candidates as the legislation remains stalled.

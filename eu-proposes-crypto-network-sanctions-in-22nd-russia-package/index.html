EU proposes crypto network sanctions in 22nd Russia package

The European Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions proposal targets crypto and payment networks, including in third countries, as it seeks to curb sanctions evasion.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 25, 2026 · 10:37 UTC#0e3c453 min read

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks it says help Russia evade sanctions, as part of its 22nd sanctions package, presented on 23 November. The Commission statement by Ursula von der Leyen also sets out a transaction ban on about 60 Russian banks and a new Crypto Sanctions Task Force.

How would the proposal reach crypto transactions?

The stated target is the network around a transaction: crypto platforms and payment networks, including those in third countries that help Russia evade existing sanctions. The Commission has not specified in its statement which platforms would be affected or the exact restrictions they would face. It says the task force will support the effort and that the EU wants to align its work with international partners.

That makes this an attempt to address routes around financial restrictions, rather than a rule described as applying to every crypto asset or user. The mechanism, as presented, is to identify and target service providers and payment links that facilitate sanctions evasion. The statement does not list those providers.

Which financial institutions would face new limits?

The proposal would extend a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use for international payments. The ban concerns transactions with those banks; SWIFT removal cuts their access to that messaging network. Both measures are proposals in the new package, not changes already in force.

Crypto services sit alongside these conventional banking measures in the package. The Commission’s focus on networks in third countries signals that it wants to address services beyond Russia when they help evade EU restrictions. The statement does not name countries or describe a test for determining which services qualify.

What changes now, and who must approve it?

The European Commission has proposed the package; EU member states must endorse it before the measures can take effect. The Associated Press report on the proposal says all 27 EU countries must approve it and that the process can take weeks, with measures potentially modified.

For crypto platforms and payment networks, the immediate development is a proposed enforcement push, backed by a task force. For banks, the proposed transaction ban and SWIFT removals would constrain designated institutions’ financial links. The package also proposes barring Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at EU sports events, banning official Russian symbols, and restricting Russian sponsors’ financial flows to European sports and cultural associations. Those measures, too, await member-state approval.

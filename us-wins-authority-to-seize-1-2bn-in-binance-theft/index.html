US wins authority to seize $1.2bn in Binance theft

US authorities won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2bn in Binance theft proceeds, part of $2.7bn frozen by government and private partners so far.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 21, 2026 · 01:40 UTC#eeeb073 min read

The FBI says US authorities have court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The Justice Department said on November 12 that the funds are part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through joint government and private-sector efforts since the theft.

The process began after North Korean military hackers, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29, according to the Justice Department’s announcement. Authorities and cryptocurrency firms then worked to stop the stolen assets from moving further. The department said the hackers continued laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

Freezing funds and getting court authority

A freeze blocks assets from being moved, while a seizure or restraint under court authority gives the government a legal route to hold them. The distinction matters: the FBI’s announcement does not say that the government has already taken final ownership of all the cryptocurrency covered by the court order. The order applies to more than $1.2 billion within the larger pool already frozen.

To interrupt transfers, law enforcement worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs. These are companies that handle cryptocurrency transactions. The FBI, US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation also issued an alert urging VASPs to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the Binance proceeds, as well as transactions derived from those addresses. The alert gives companies a way to identify and stop funds as they pass through services.

That intervention resembles putting a hold on a payment while it is still moving through a chain of providers. The blockchain records transfers between addresses, but the Justice Department says the actors have used bridges to move assets between networks, mixers to obscure transaction trails, and exchanges to move funds through trading services. Investigators are working to trace the assets across those routes.

Forfeiture remains a next step

The seizure effort is ongoing. The FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit are coordinating recovery work with the Secret Service and IRS-CI, the Justice Department said. Authorities have not said how much of the more than $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization has been transferred into government custody.

As the Associated Press reported, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge in such cases. The judge then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The court authorization gives investigators legal power to secure assets while that process continues; it does not itself resolve who ultimately receives them.

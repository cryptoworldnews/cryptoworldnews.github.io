How to Swap for GHO on Ethereum and See It in Your Wallet

How to buy GHO stablecoin on Ethereum: swap ETH or USDC through a wallet, verify Aave’s token address, and check that GHO appears in your balance.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 02:23 UTC#f21fba4 min read

Swap ETH or USDC for GHO on Ethereum, and the tokens should appear in your self-custody wallet after the transaction confirms. You’ll need ETH for gas, even when USDC is the token you swap.

A wallet swap buys GHO from on-chain liquidity

A DEX swap exchanges tokens you already own for GHO through on-chain liquidity pools; Uniswap and CoW Swap are two services you can use to find a route. Think of the swap as exchanging one item for another at a quoted rate: the pool or aggregator calculates how much GHO your ETH or USDC can receive, then a transaction changes your wallet balances.

Borrowing GHO on Aave is a different route. Aave mints GHO against eligible collateral, and the borrowed amount becomes debt that accrues interest. A swap buys GHO with tokens you hold; it does not create an Aave loan.

Keep slippage low when setting up the trade. GHO is a stablecoin designed to track the U.S. dollar, so its market price should sit near $1, though the rate you receive can vary with liquidity, fees and market conditions. A large slippage setting can allow a worse execution than you intended.

Follow the Ethereum swap from token address to wallet balance

Start with a self-custody wallet connected to Ethereum mainnet and funded with ETH for gas, plus ETH or USDC to exchange. The steps below take you from that starting balance to GHO in the wallet. Aave staking is a separate protocol activity that may interest readers exploring Aave after the swap; it is not part of buying GHO.

  1. Copy GHO’s contract address from Aave’s docs. Use the Ethereum mainnet address listed for GHO, rather than searching for the token by name in the swap screen. Lookalike tokens can use the same name or ticker, so the contract address is the identifier that ties the token to the one documented by Aave. Copy it directly and keep it ready to paste.
  2. Open a DEX or aggregator and connect your wallet. Use a service such as Uniswap or CoW Swap, then confirm the site shows Ethereum mainnet and the wallet account you intend to use. A different network may show a different token or route, and the transaction requires ETH on Ethereum to pay gas.
  3. Paste the address as the output token. Choose ETH or USDC as the token you are selling, then paste GHO’s contract address into the token you want to receive. If the interface asks you to add a custom token, compare the displayed address with the one copied from Aave’s docs before continuing.
  4. Enter the amount and review the quote. Check the estimated GHO output, the exchange rate, fees and slippage before signing. For a USDC swap, the wallet may ask you to approve the token first; that approval lets the swap contract use the specified USDC. Review the spender and amount in your wallet, and remember that the approval and the swap may be separate transactions, each requiring gas.
  5. Confirm the swap, then check the result. Sign the swap transaction in your wallet and wait for it to confirm on Ethereum. If GHO does not appear, add it as a custom token using the same Aave contract address. Aave staking is a separate choice after you hold eligible assets; the swap itself only puts GHO in your wallet.

When the GHO balance is visible under the correct Ethereum account, the purchase is complete. The balance display is the final check that the received token is the one associated with Aave’s address.

  • Copy GHO’s Ethereum address from Aave’s docs.
  • Swap ETH or USDC on Ethereum and review the quote.
  • Confirm the transaction and add GHO as a custom token if needed.
  • Check that the GHO balance appears in your wallet.

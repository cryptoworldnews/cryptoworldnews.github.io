Common mistakes when bridging crypto: a pre-signing checklist

Common mistakes when bridging crypto include a wrong address, network or token, missing destination gas, and fake sites. Run these checks before signing.

Merkle Street NewsroomNov 20, 2026 · 16:01 UTC#d7a6424 min read

The most common mistakes when bridging crypto are entering the wrong recipient, choosing the wrong network or token, arriving without destination gas, and signing on a fake site. A confirmed blockchain transfer cannot be reversed, so each check belongs before you approve the transaction.

A bridge moves assets between blockchains through a transfer process: you choose a source chain and asset, provide a destination address, then approve the transaction in your wallet. The Berachain bridge is a cross-chain asset bridge for moving assets into Berachain’s DeFi ecosystem; its overview adds context about that destination.

Which checks prevent common mistakes when bridging crypto?

Run these four checks against the bridge form and your wallet before signing. Each catches a mistake that can leave funds at the wrong address, on the wrong chain, or unusable.

  • Recipient address: Copy the destination address from the wallet you intend to use, then compare every character with the address shown in the bridge form. Do not copy it from transaction history. Attackers can send tiny “dust” transfers from addresses that look almost identical to yours. That fake address then appears in your history, where it can be mistaken for a familiar recipient. Say you see a recent transfer to an address beginning and ending like your own: that resemblance is not proof it belongs to you. Open your wallet, copy the address there, and compare the full string before signing.
  • Network and token: Confirm the source network selected in the bridge matches the chain where your assets are held, and confirm the token is the one you mean to send. A familiar token name alone is not enough; check the asset and network labels in the form. If you select the wrong source, the bridge may not be acting on the funds you intended to move.
  • Destination gas: Make sure you will have the destination chain’s native token to pay for transactions after the transfer. Gas is paid in the native token of each chain: ETH on Ethereum and BERA on Berachain. Bridging ETH to Berachain without any BERA can leave the transferred funds unusable for wallet actions until you obtain BERA. The bridge transfer and the later transaction are separate steps; funds arriving does not pay the next transaction’s gas.
  • Site URL: Open the bridge from an official project source you already trust, and inspect the full URL before connecting your wallet. A search ad can lead to a fake site that copies the real bridge’s appearance. A common misunderstanding is that a familiar logo or a top search result proves a site is official. It does not: the URL is the detail to verify. If anything in the address is unexpected, do not connect or sign.

Related questions

What should I do if I sent to the wrong address?

A confirmed transfer to the wrong address cannot be reversed on the blockchain. That is why the recipient check matters before signing; this checklist covers prevention, not recovery.

Do I need a new wallet to use another chain?

Usually, no. A wallet such as MetaMask can work with multiple supported networks. Select the intended network in the wallet and make sure the bridge form shows the same source or destination chain you intend to use.

How do I prepare gas and verify the bridge before transferring?

How do I get gas on a new chain before bridging?

Arrange a small amount of that chain’s native token through a service or transfer route that supports the destination chain. Confirm the wallet address and network, then check that the token arrived before relying on it for later transactions. On Berachain, that gas token is BERA.

How do I verify an official bridge URL?

Start from the project’s official website or a trusted official channel, follow its bridge link, and read the full address bar before connecting. Compare the domain character by character. Avoid relying on ads, search ranking, logos, or a link copied from an unfamiliar message.
